This KitchenAid dish rack is designed to hold dishware like plates, bowls, cookware, and serveware upright for efficient drainage and drying. As clean dishes in the rack dry, they drip water onto a sloping drainboard that users can orient toward the sink to avoid a mess on the countertop.

The dish rack features a removable utensil caddy on one end, which holds regular cutlery as well as sharp kitchen knives and cooking utensils that are best not run through the dishwasher. It also offers narrow wire loops on the other end of the rack that hang over the sink to hold cups and glasses upside down for quick drying. Simple Setup

While several of the other dish racks I tested had multiple parts to fit together and were made with flimsier materials, the KitchenAid dish rack was very straightforward to set up. I didn’t need tools or any special instructions to assemble the rack, though a simple guide is included. Easy to Use

I found the KitchenAid dish rack to be very well designed and user-friendly. The center of the rack features loops that keep small and large dinner plates apart. The utensil caddy has three slots to separate sharp, delicate blades from everyday forks and knives, and it clips firmly to the side of the rack so I didn’t need to balance it out with equally weighted items. Both the caddy and the drainboard were easy for me to remove and then put right back into place.

Because this dish rack is large, it did get quite heavy when full of dishes. The rack also does not have a rotating drainage lip. Though it will work on either side of the sink, shoppers with an unusual kitchen design could find that this rack doesn’t work with their setup. Sturdy Construction

The frame of the KitchenAid dish rack is constructed of strong, rust-resistant stainless steel, and the drainboard and utensil caddy are made of durable plastic. The dividers are sturdy enough to hold heavy cast-iron cookware, and the plastic won’t bend or warp under high heat (such as in the dishwasher) or pressure.

The rack’s rubber feet kept it in place, so my dishes didn’t crash together if the rack was lightly jostled on the counter or I accidentally bumped into it.
