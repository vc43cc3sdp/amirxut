If you’re struggling to find a dedicated workspace in your home to pay bills or file reports, look no further than your hallway or closet. Homeowners can very easily transform small spaces such as the attic or a room’s corner into a desk without sacrificing square footage. Read our guide below for eight desk ideas that are perfect for small spaces. 8 Desk Ideas Perfect for Small Spaces

Consider space constraints and work needs before diving into more specific desk ideas. Once you’ve nailed down the basics, consider some of the ideas below. The Underused Closet Veronica Rodriguez/GAP Photos

Transforming an underused closet into a workspace has become increasingly popular since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic. “The cloffice” also has the advantage of freeing up space elsewhere in your home. Consider one of the variations on this trend below.

Fold-away design: Install a fold-down desk that you can easily tuck away.

Full conversion: Remove closet doors and install a desk, shelving, and adequate lighting.

Partial conversion: Add a compact desk area while still keeping some closet space for storage.

Pro Tip: For a full conversion, consider covering the walls, ceiling, and floor of the closet with plywood cut into planks to give the nook a finished, intentional look—as one This Old House Magazine featured homeowner did for her Craftsman cottage. Casing the opening of the alcove with leftover plywood, MDF scraps, or the wall’s old baseboards helps the workspace blend seamlessly into the room rather than looking like, well, a closet.

A shelf-style desk with overhead storage shelves works especially well for smaller closets. Be sure to upgrade your lighting with LED lights under shelves or a multi-bulb chandelier.

Pro Tip: According to This Old House Magazine’s closet-to-office guide, if your closet doors don’t fold back, take advantage of the vertical real estate by screwing thin sheets of cork to their back sides for posting notes and bills. Also, set shelves at least 1 foot apart and mount them with standards, which can hold up to 70 pounds per foot. And don’t forget to install a power strip behind the desk cubbies to conceal cords—an essential step that keeps the small workspace from looking cluttered. The Bay Window Douglas Gibb/GAP Photos

A bay window creates desk space that features abundant natural light and an open feel. It can also improve your mood and productivity. Follow the steps below to set up a bay window desk:

Measure the window space and cut a piece of wood or countertop to fit.

Install the desktop using brackets or a floating shelf system.

Choose a chair that complements your decor and can double as extra seating.

The Corner

Corner spaces are perfect for compact work areas, and won’t clutter your room. Below are some ideas for maximizing corner space:

Custom-cut corner desk: Install a triangular desktop to fit snugly in the corner.

Floating corner shelves: Install tiered shelves above the desk for additional storage.

L-shaped desk: Use a pre-made, L-shaped desk to maximize corner space.

Pro Tip: Designers generally recommend a desk height of 28 to 30 inches and a knee-hole depth of 25 inches if you plan to sit down for long periods. If you prefer to check email standing, work surfaces can be built up to 42 inches high. And if space is truly tight, you could fit a computer station into an 18-inch-wide cubby—but if you want to sit, expand it to at least 21 inches wide.

Add a slide-out keyboard tray and under-cabinet lighting to improve your desk’s functionality. Consider LED lighting to illuminate your corner workspace. Fold-Down Wall Desk Mark Scott/GAP Photos

A fold-down wall desk saves space, and works well in bedrooms, hallways, or even kitchens. These desks keep your living spaces tidy, and are ideal for homeowners who prefer minimalist aesthetics. Make the most of your space by following these tips:

Attach artwork or a corkboard to the underside of the desk to generate visual interest when you fold the desk up.

Choose a desk with built-in storage compartments for office supplies.

Install shelving above the desk for additional storage. Ladder Desk

Ladder desks feature a ladder-like design that leans against the wall, and features shelves instead of rungs. The lowest shelves serve as the desktop, and the upper shelves provide storage for books, plants, and decor. The benefits of these desk types are below.

Benefits of ladder desks include the following:

Available in various styles and finishes to match your decor Minimal floor space requirements Vertical storage options

Take proper measurements of your space before proceeding with this type of desk, and make sure it can fit snugly.

Pro Tip: As This Old House Magazine contributor Cody Calamaio noted, a ladder bookshelf “conveniently sidesteps baseboard moldings and receptacles, and with just two feet to level, it’s not so easily upset by those charming warped floors.” The same advantage applies to ladder desks—their lean-back design means you won’t have to fuss with uneven walls or protruding trim.
