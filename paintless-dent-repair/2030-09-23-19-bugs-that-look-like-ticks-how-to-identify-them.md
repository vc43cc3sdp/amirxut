Ticks aren’t always easy to recognize. With several lookalikes out there, it’s possible to mistake another insect for a tick. Knowing which bugs look like ticks can help you correctly identify them. We spoke with pest control experts for insight on bugs commonly mistaken for ticks and what distinguishing features to look for. In this guide, discover the bugs that resemble ticks and learn how to identify an actual tick. What bugs look like ticks? Download Image Download Image

Pest control specialist Kevin Carrillo says bed bugs, spider beetles, cockroach nymphs, and odd beetles are some insects commonly mistaken for ticks. These bugs have similar physical characteristics, like oval-shaped bodies and a reddish/brownish color. However, ticks have eight legs, while other insects have six legs. Bed Bugs According to Carrillo, people commonly mistake bed bugs for ticks.[1]XExpert SourceKevin Carrillo

Their favorite places to burrow are in mattresses and bedding. They also leave behind dark stains and shed skin.[3]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature:Bed bugs have six legs, whereas ticks have eight.

Spider Beetles Spider beetles are another insect that looks similar to ticks, says Carrillo.[5]XExpert SourceKevin Carrillo

Pest Control SpecialistExpert Interview They’re usually a deep red color and measure about 1⁄16 to 3⁄16 inch (0.16 to 0.48 cm) long. They have a round, bubble-shaped abdomen that makes them look like a spider. Since they like to feed on dry goods, they can be found in pantries, between wooden boards, and even in warehouses.[6]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Spider beetles have long antennae on their heads that can be mistaken for another pair of legs. Advertisement 3

Baby Cockroaches Cockroach nymphs can closely resemble ticks, notes Carrillo.[7]XExpert SourceKevin Carrillo

Pest Control SpecialistExpert Interview This is because they have a flat, oval shape and dark brownish color. Size can vary depending on the species, but they typically measure about 1⁄8 inch (0.32 cm) long as babies. Cockroaches are known to be annoying household pests, and if you spot a baby cockroach, that usually indicates an infestation.[8]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Baby cockroaches move fast, unlike the slower-moving ticks. 4

Odd Beetles Odd beetles are another insect that is commonly mistaken for ticks, explains Carrillo.[9]XExpert SourceKevin Carrillo

Pest Control SpecialistExpert Interview Also known as tissue paper beetles, these beetles are tiny, measuring around 1–4 millimetres (0.039–0.157 in) long.[10]XResearch source They are a yellowish-brown color, with females more closely resembling ticks, as they have a similar rounded body, while males are more distinct with a long body and wings. Odd beetles are notorious pests in museums.[11]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Odd beetles are a yellowish-brown color and have six legs. Males exclusively have wings and an elongated body. 5

WeevilsWeevils are a type of beetle that measure about 1⁄4 inch (0.64 cm). The shape of their bodies is like a pear or a light bulb. They have six legs, antennae, and some have wings. They can be reddish-brown or black and have a hard shell with tiny holes. Unlike ticks, they feed on crops and can be found among crop fields, plants, and in cereal grains.[12]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Weevils have wings and can fly. 6

Clover Mites The clover mite is an arachnid with eight legs, like a tick. However, they’re much smaller and around 1⁄30 inch (0.085 cm) long. They come out during the spring and fall and are drawn to sunny areas in your home, like window sills. They feed on plants and don’t pose the same risks as ticks.[13]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: They’re bright red and when squished, their bodies release a red pigment. This isn’t blood, but their body fluid. 7

Brown Marmorated Stink Bugs The brown marmorated stink bug has reddish-orange markings that make some people think they’re a tick. They grow to about 1⁄2 to 5⁄8 inch (1.3 to 1.6 cm) in length and have a wide, triangular-shaped body. They release a foul odor when they’re squished and are commonly found on plants and crops.[14]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Brown marmorated stink bugs have wings.

Brown marmorated stink bugs usually get into homes during the winter months for warmth. 8

Carpet BeetlesCarpet beetles have a pair of antennae on their heads and measure about 1⁄16 to 1⁄8 inch (0.16 to 0.32 cm) in length. They’re oval-shaped, and some have a brownish or orange color that can make them look similar to a tick. Unlike ticks, these bugs prefer to feed on fabrics instead of blood. Their diet consists of textiles, like wool, cotton, and leather, and they can usually be spotted on the undersides of rugs and carpets.[15]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Carpet beetles have six legs. 9

FleasFleas are around 1⁄8 inch (0.32 cm) long and, similar to ticks, like to make themselves at home on pets. Like most insects, fleas have six legs and two antennae, which ticks don’t have. They have narrow bodies and cause bites on humans and animals that itch.[16]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Fleas jump, whereas ticks only crawl. 10

Black Aphids There are many species of aphids, but black aphids tend to get more frequently mistaken for ticks. They measure about 1⁄8 inch (0.32 cm) in length and have soft, pear-shaped bodies. Some adults may also have wings. Their diet consists of plant sap, so they can be spotted on stems, leaves, and roots.[17]XResearch source

Deer Keds Deer keds have flat bodies that are dark brown in color. They grow to about 0.28 inches (0.71 cm) long, which is about the size of a grain of rice. They’re blood-sucking insects that typically feed on deer, but they also bite other animals and even humans. They’re born with wings, but once they find a host and latch on, they lose their wings, which can make it more difficult to tell them apart from a tick.[18]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Deer keds have six legs. 12

Pseudoscorpions A pseudoscorpion has an oval-shaped, dark brown body. It measures about 0.2 inches (0.51 cm) long and looks a lot like an unfed tick. These insects prefer to stay outside, so it’s not as common to find them in your home. They don't carry disease, and they feed on tiny bugs, such as lice and ants.[19]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Pseudoscorpions have two distinct pincers located at the front. 13

Larder Beetles The larder beetle has an oval shape and dark brown body. It grows to between 1⁄4 to 1⁄3 inch (0.64 to 0.85 cm) in length. Spring is when these bugs enter homes through open spaces and gaps. They’re drawn to areas containing dry food, such as pantries, and have been known to infest dry pet kibble.[20]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Larder beetles have a cream or yellow-colored band with dark spots across the tops of their wings. 14

Head LiceHead lice have long, slender bodies and six legs. They’re tiny and are about 2 to 3 millimetres (0.079 to 0.118 in) long with nymphs being even smaller. They’re translucent when they haven’t fed but turn dark once they’ve had their fill of blood, which is when they’re mostly likely to be mistaken for ticks.[21]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Head lice are most commonly found on a person’s scalp. Unlike ticks who usually fall off their host after feeding, head lice remain and require over-the-counter treatments to remove. 15

Harlequin Bugs Harlequin bugs have an oval shape and grow to 1⁄4 to 3⁄8 inch (0.64 to 0.95 cm) in length. Adults emerge in the springtime and are often found among plants and in vegetable gardens. They enjoy feeding on cabbage, kale, stock, and crucifers and suck plant juices from leaves.[22]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Harlequin bugs have vibrant red, orange, and yellow markings. 16

Deer Flies Deer flies measure about 1⁄4 to 1⁄2 inch (0.64 to 1.27 cm) long and have slim antennae that are longer than their head. They’re usually confused with ticks because they also feed on blood, biting livestock and humans. It’s unlikely to find these insects in your home as they prefer to stay in moist environments, like marshes, ponds, and streams.[23]XResearch source

[Bugs That](https://bugs-that.themaplelane.com/bugs-that/20260826.html)

Distinguishing feature: Deer flies have wings and fly around. They also bite a host and fly off, as opposed to latching on. 17

Drugstore Beetle Drugstore beetles have a reddish-brown color with a length of around 1⁄8 to 1⁄10 inch (0.32 to 0.25 cm). Their bodies also have a cylindrical shape to them. They’re considered food pests that like to feed on grains, pet food, straw flowers, tea, spices, and dried plant material. You can usually tell that you have a drugstore beetle infestation because they make holes in food packaging and leave behind dusty particles inside, which is their waste.[24]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: They have red and black eyes, six legs, and antennae. 18

Hog Louse Hog louse are a type of lice that feed on pigs. They’re around the size of a sesame seed with an estimated length of 1⁄4 inch (0.64 cm). Like ticks, they attach themselves onto their hosts to drink blood and can cause skin irritation and spread disease.[25]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature: Hog louse have long heads, as well as six legs and antennae. 19

Crab Spiders Crab spiders have flat, segmented bodies that measure at around 2 to 11 millimetres (0.079 to 0.433 in) long. Since they’re also arachnids with eight legs, you may think one is a tick at a quick glance. They come in different colors, like light gray, dark brown, and black. They’re usually found in gardens and landscapes and feed on pest insects as opposed to pets or humans.[26]XResearch source

Distinguishing feature:Crab spiders resemble crabs and can move sideways. They also have eight small eyes. Advertisement Section 2 of 2: How to Identify Ticks Download Article

Ticks are arachnids that feed on blood. One of the key ways to tell adult ticks apart from insects is that ticks have eight legs, similar to spiders, explains Carrillo. On the other hand, all other common insects only have six legs. He adds that it’s much easier to tell a tick apart from another insect if it has fed, because it will become bloated with blood.[27]XExpert SourceKevin Carrillo Pest Control SpecialistExpert Interview

Pest control specialist Elmer Bensinger explains that, while there are different types of ticks, they all have four legs on each side of their bodies, totaling eight legs. They also have a long snout on the front called a mandible and a flat, round body, he adds.[28]XExpert SourceElmer Bensinger Pest Control SpecialistExpert Interview

Ticks only crawl and cannot fly or jump. They often hide in vegetation, then jump on a host, like dogs or other animals.[29]XTrustworthy SourceCenters for Disease Control and PreventionMain public health institute for the US, run by the Dept. of Health and Human ServicesGo to source

To help determine if you’re looking at a tick or not, Carrillo suggests taking a clear picture and emailing it to a local pest control provider. He doesn’t recommend relying on an AI image search, as it may provide a false identification.[30]XExpert SourceKevin Carrillo Pest Control SpecialistExpert Interview
