Stone veneer is an increasingly popular choice for homeowners wanting to remodel their exterior or living space. It’s more budget-friendly than natural stone, allowing you to capture the same look at a fraction of the price. Whether you’re considering a new rustic fireplace or completing an exterior remodel, we’ll explain the basics of stone veneer types, installation, maintenance, and more. What Is Stone Veneer?

Stone veneer is a manmade material designed to mimic the appearance of natural stone. It’s a thin layer of stone or stone-like material that can be applied to various surfaces on the interior or exterior of your home. Stone veneer offers the beauty and texture of natural stone without the structural requirements and high costs associated with full-stone masonry.

There are two main types of stone veneer: natural and manufactured. Natural stone veneer is cut from actual stone, while manufactured stone veneer is created from concrete and other materials to replicate the look of natural stone. Both types offer unique benefits and can be used in a wide range of interior and exterior applications.

Natural stone veneer is cut from actual stone to a thickness of 3/4 inch to 14 inches, while manufactured stone veneer is created from a mixture of Portland cement, lightweight aggregate (such as pumice), and iron-oxide pigments shaped in a mold. Natural Stone Veneer

Natural stone veneer is cut directly from real stone, typically ranging from 3/4 inch to 1 1/4 inches thick. Natural stone veneer is a good choice for a more authentic stone look, and despite the higher cost than manufacturer veneer, it’s still cheaper than using real stone. Here are some pros and cons:

Better durability compared to manufactured veneer Natural variations in color and texture Higher cost than manufactured veneer Manufactured Stone Veneer

Manufactured stone veneer, or cultured or cast stone, is made from a concrete mixture with added pigments and is shaped in molds created from real stones. Manufactured veneer has improved significantly in recent years, with more attention to detail, making it nearly indistinguishable from natural stone. Key benefits include the following:

Ability to closely replicate natural stone

Lighter weight than natural stone veneer

More affordable than natural stone options

The manufacturing process involves mixing Portland cement with lightweight pumice and mineral oxides for color and texture. The mixture is then shaped in molds to create pieces that can be less than 2 inches thick, making installation significantly easier than traditional stone work.

Take time to compare samples and visualize how different stone veneer options will look in your space before making a decision. Consider the following factors:

Style and color: Choose a veneer that complements your home’s architecture and existing color scheme.

Application area: Consider whether the veneer will be used indoors or outdoors, as this may affect your choice of material.

Budget: Determine how much you’re willing to spend, as prices can vary significantly between natural and manufactured options.

Maintenance requirements: Some types of stone veneer may require more upkeep than others, so it’s important to account for the time required to maintain the veneer and increase its longevity.

Local climate: If you’re installing stone veneer somewhere outdoors, whether on your home or along the entry path, ensure it can withstand your area’s weather conditions.

Installing stone veneer requires careful preparation and attention to detail for long-lasting, realistic-looking results. While some homeowners may opt for DIY installation, we generally recommend professional installation, especially for exterior applications. DIY Installation Tips

Most homeowners can install stone veneer themselves when working on small interior projects. Always do your research on techniques and preparation before starting. If you’re considering a DIY stone veneer project, keep these tips in mind:

Always check local building codes before starting your project.

Hide cut edges by placing them above or below eye level when possible.

Install corner pieces first, then work from the top down to prevent mortar stains.

Lay out your design on the ground before installing it to achieve a fitting pattern. Professional Installation

For exterior or large-scale installations, hiring a professional is usually best. Professional installation can save time and prevent mistakes, especially for more complex or involved projects. Professional installers have the experience and tools necessary to ensure these results:

Correct application of moisture barriers and lath

Even distribution of mortar and precise placement of stones Proper preparation of the substrate

Proper sealing and finishing for long-lasting results Cost Considerations for Stone Veneers

Cost Comparison: Natural vs. Manufactured

As mentioned, natural stone veneer is usually more expensive, costing an average of $11–$18 per square foot. In contrast, manufactured stone veneer ranges from $5–$8 per square foot.* Installation costs typically add around $2–$4 or more per square foot for either type. Though pricier, natural stone often lasts longer than its manufactured counterpart.

Stone veneer is relatively low-maintenance compared to other building materials, but you should still take the following steps to keep it looking great. Boralna | Photo by Boralna Cleaning Methods For routine cleaning:

Avoid harsh chemicals or pressure washing, which can damage the stone or mortar.

A mild solution of water and white vinegar can be effective for efflorescence (white, powdery deposits).

Light dusting or rinsing with a hose is often sufficient for both interior and exterior applications.

Use a soft-bristled brush for more stubborn dirt. Repair and Touch-ups

Regular inspections can address any issues before they become more serious problems, but touch-ups may be needed at some point, especially for exterior stone veneer. Minor damage to stone veneer can often be addressed with the following repairs:

Touch-up kits are available to fix small chips or scratches on manufactured stones.

Loose stones can be re-adhered with appropriate mortar.

For more significant damage, individual stones may need to be replaced.

Durability and Longevity of Stone Veneer

When properly installed and maintained, stone veneer can be a long-lasting addition to your home. Natural stone veneer is generally more durable than manufactured options, but both can provide decades of beauty with proper care.

Manufactured stone veneer may be more prone to chipping or cracking than natural stone. However, advancements in production techniques have significantly improved its durability in recent years.

Quality of installation, exposure to elements, maintenance and cleaning frequency, and the type of stone veneer used can all impact the longevity of your new stone veneer. Pros and Cons of Stone Veneer

Comparing Stone Veneer to Other Materials

Stone veneer provides a great balance of customization, cost, and ease of installation, but it’s not your only option. Here are some other materials you may want to consider:

Brick: More uniform appearance, potentially lower cost.

Stucco: Smoother texture, less dimensional than stone veneer.

Fiber cement siding: More color options, less textural interest.

Natural stone: More authentic but significantly heavier and more expensive.

Environmental Impact and Sustainability of Stone Veneers

Stone veneer’s environmental impact depends on the type you use. Natural stone veneer requires quarrying (removing rock, sand, gravel, etc. from the ground for construction), but it’s a more natural, long-lasting material. Manufactured stone veneer uses fewer natural resources but involves energy-intensive production processes.

Some manufacturers offer recycled content in their products, and stone veneer’s longevity can contribute to its overall sustainability. Additionally, its lightweight nature can reduce transportation emissions compared to full stone.
