# How To Build a Bunk Bed - This Old House

Building a bunk bed is a practical DIY project to help save space and add a fun element to any bedroom. With proper planning and execution, you can create a safe and sturdy bunk bed that will last for years. This guide will take you through the process of designing and constructing your own custom bunk bed, from initial planning to final touches. Planning Your DIY Bunk Bed Project

This phase involves assessing your space, selecting materials, and gathering the necessary tools. Assessing Room Dimensions

Start by measuring the room where the bunk bed will go. Consider factors such as: Ceiling height Door and window locations Electrical outlets and light switches Traffic flow in the room

Mark the bed’s footprint on the floor with blue painter’s tape. This will help you visualize its placement.

Pro Tip: When Nikki and Caleb Grandy of San Tan Valley, Arizona, needed to fit three boys into one shared bedroom, they discovered that three twin beds consumed much of the room’s total area. By carefully measuring their space and switching to built-in bunks anchored into the walls and ceiling with a 2×4 frame, the couple was able to leave the floor clear for spreading out toys and roughhousing. Their project, featured in This Old House Magazine, is a great reminder that assessing your room dimensions first can open up creative solutions you might not have considered. Choosing Materials

Select durable materials that can withstand the weight and wear of regular use. Here are some common options:

Solid wood (such as pine, oak, or maple) Plywood for platforms Metal for additional support

When choosing your wood, look for straight, knot-free boards to ensure structural integrity. Gathering Necessary Tools

Gather the following tools before beginning construction: Circular saw or table saw Drill and drill bits Level Measuring tape Sander

Safety equipment (goggles, gloves, dust mask) Screwdriver Taking Safety Precautions

Safety should be top-of-mind when building a bunk bed, especially one for children. Key safety features include the following:

Adequate space between the top bunk and ceiling Guardrails on all sides of the top bunk Secure ladder attachment Sturdy construction to support weight

Always follow safety guidelines and local building codes when constructing your bunk bed. Designing Your Bunk Bed

A well-thought-out design is essential for a safe, functional bunk bed. Choosing a Type of Bunk Bed

Bunk beds come in various styles to suit different needs and preferences. Here are a few options:

Standard bunk beds: Two beds stacked vertically

L-shaped bunk beds: Two beds arranged perpendicular to each other

Loft beds: A single elevated bed with space underneath for a desk or storage

Triple bunk beds: Three beds stacked vertically or in an L-shape Determining Bed Height

How much room do you want between the top of the mattress and the ceiling? The general rule is that someone should be able to sit up, reasonably move around, and climb up and down the ladder easily. A 9-foot ceiling is ideal for a bed like this. Here are some other considerations:

Allow at least 33 inches between the top mattress and ceiling to sit up comfortably

Ensure the bottom bunk has enough headroom (typically 36 inches minimum)

Account for mattress thickness in your measurements

Use a piece of cardboard or tape to mark the proposed height on the wall to help you visualize the final product. Picking Ladder Placement

Consider the following when planning where to place the ladder: Allow enough space for easy climbing

It shouldn’t interfere with the room’s traffic flow

Integrating the ladder into the bed’s structure can add stability Choosing Design Accents

Design accents can make your bunk bed more appealing and tailored to the room’s décor. Consider using themed colors and patterns, or adding decorative elements like carvings or decals. You can even incorporate innovative shapes for headboards and footboards. These touches can transform a functional bunk bed into a stylish centerpiece of the room. Incorporating Additional Features

Extra features can make your bunk bed multifunctional, perfect for maximizing space in smaller rooms. These include built-in storage drawers, shelving for books or personal items, or a desk area beneath a loft-style bed.

Step-by-Step Construction Process of a Bunk Bed

With your design finalized, it’s time to start building. The below instructions are for a simple built-in design using commodity lumber. Building the Bed Structure

This type of bunk bed has a lot in common with deck framing. Get started with a ledger board, which needs to be cut to a specific length:

[To Build Bunk](https://to-build-bunk.northlist.shop/to-build-bunk/20260822380.html)

If your bed is 8 feet long and you’re building the frame from 2-by-1 1/2-inch thick lumber, cut three inches off the ledger. Later, you’ll fasten the left and right sides of the mattress box to it, and the whole piece will be eight feet long.

Locate the studs and fasten your ledger in place with two 3-inch deck screws per stud. Make sure it’s level.

Install the left, right and front of the box frame. Use temporary posts to hold the left and right pieces level while you install the permanent posts. You can make posts using 2-by-6 boards fastened at right angles to each other to form an “L” shape. If there’s carpet underneath, compress it so that the box frame remains level once the weight is applied.

Square up the box by taking diagonal measurements of the bed frame and jimmying the frame’s outer corners until your measurements are equal. Install a 2-by-2 cleat inside the mattress box. Attaching Safety Rails

Next, install the bed rail, which is an essential safety feature:

Cut rails to extend at least five inches above the mattress surface.

Attach the rails securely to the bed frame using bolts or screws.

Ensure there are no gaps larger than 4 1/2 inches between rails to prevent entrapment.

Test the rails by applying pressure. They should withstand the weight of a person leaning against them.

Pro Tip: When installing balusters between rails, keep the spacing to 4 inches or less for safety. Drill pilot holes into both the top and bottom rails sized to match the baluster pins—for example, use a paddle bit to enlarge pilot holes to the correct diameter and depth. Apply wood glue to the baluster pins and fit them into the bottom rail’s holes first, squaring up the faces before the glue dries. Then fit the top rail onto the balusters, using a mallet and a piece of scrap wood to protect the rail’s surface while tapping it into a snug fit. Creating a Sturdy Ladder

You’ll need to build a sturdy ladder to make the bed easily accessible. You can make it from clear, 3/4-inch-thick pine or oak. Here’s how to build one that’s both functional and secure:

Cut two side rails to the appropriate length, allowing the ladder to extend slightly above the top bunk for easy access.

Cut rungs to fit between the side rails, spacing them 10–12 inches apart.

Attach the rungs to the side rails using wood glue and screws for added strength. Sand all surfaces to prevent splinters.

Test the ladder’s sturdiness before attaching it to the bed frame. Securing the Ladder

For safety reasons, you should screw the top of the ladder to the bed frame:

Position the ladder at a slight angle for easier climbing.

Attach the top of the ladder securely to the bed frame using bolts or heavy-duty brackets.

Consider adding non-slip pads to the bottom of the ladder to prevent sliding.

Test the ladder to make sure it’s stable and doesn’t wobble.

Pro Tip: When fastening rungs or treads, always pre-drill your screw holes to prevent the wood from splitting—especially important with hardwoods like oak. As demonstrated in a This Old House project, using two screws per joint through the outer face of each rail and into the ends of each rung creates a solid, long-lasting connection.

Adding Finishing Touches to Your Bunk Bed Project

Finally, add the finishing touches for a polished result. Sanding and Smoothing

A well-sanded surface looks better and is safer for kids using the bed. Thoroughly sand all surfaces of the bunk bed for a smooth finish:

Start with coarse-grit sandpaper to remove any rough spots.

Progress to finer grits for a polished surface.

Pay special attention to edges and corners to prevent splinters. Applying Paint or Stain

Set aside a day to apply the finish. Urethane, boiled linseed oil, or semi-gloss paint all make great finishes. Choose a finish that complements your room decor:

For a natural look, apply a wood stain followed by a clear protective coat.

If painting, use a primer first, then apply two or three coats of paint.

Consider using non-toxic, child-safe finishes if the bed is for young children.

Allow ample drying time between coats for the best results.

If you plan to stain the bunk bed rather than paint it, consider raising the grain first by spraying the sanded surfaces with distilled water—tap or well water may contain minerals that can stain the wood. After the wood dries, give it a very light sanding with 220-grit sandpaper, just enough to knock the grain back down. Then tack off the dust before applying the stain. Water-based stains and finishes tend to raise wood grain as well, so you may need to repeat this light sanding between coats until the surface feels smooth and you’re satisfied with the coverage.

Common Bunk Bed Construction Errors and How To Avoid Them

Even with a detailed guide, mistakes can happen. Being aware of common construction errors can help you avoid them:

Incorrect measurements: Always double-check your measurements before cutting your materials.

Using inadequate fasteners: Ensure you use screws and bolts appropriate for the weight they need to support.

Skipping sanding: Never skip sanding. It prevents splinters and gives a polished finish.
