After constructing the tiki bar, you can paint it with tropical colors, add a weathered finish for a more rustic feel, install a built-in sound system to play island-themed music, or weatherproof it to withstand the elements.

Rather than simply nailing plywood together, This Old House’s bar build calls for cutting ½-inch plywood panels to size for the front and sides, then lining each panel’s perimeter with 1×4 framing boards. Arrange three more 1×4s equally spaced between the side boards for structure. Apply construction adhesive to the back of each 1×4 and nail them in place using a pneumatic nail gun and 1-inch nails. Then sheathe the exterior with lauan, finish-face out, for a clean look. Mark the location of the vertical boards on the edges of the horizontal boards so you can pinpoint them once they’re covered. Add Greenery to Your Bar Space

If you live in a climate that supports tropical flora, you can incorporate palms, bromeliads, or ferns into your landscaping. These plants can enhance the tropical vibe in your backyard and provide natural shade and privacy.

Potted plants are an excellent alternative for homeowners who live in cooler climates or have limited ground space. Large planters filled with birds of paradise, banana plants, or colorful crotons can add instant tropical flair, while hanging plants, such as orchids or staghorn ferns, can create visual interest at different heights.

For a vertical gardening option, consider adding a living wall of tropical plants to serve as a stunning backdrop for your tiki bar. You can also use bamboo screens or trellises covered in vines to create natural dividers in your backyard.

Additionally, you can plant tropical herbs and spices, such as ginger and turmeric, in your garden and use them in your tiki-inspired cocktails or dishes.

This Old House landscape contractor Roger Cook points out that containers are ideal for patios and hardscaped bar areas where you can’t dig into the ground: “It’s really hard to dig a hole through concrete and flagstone… So that’s where containers come into play.” He also notes that tropical favorites like banana plants can thrive in containers in cooler climates—just cut them back and store them in the basement for the winter. Invest in Tiki Lawn Furniture

For tropical-themed entertaining, consider blending British Colonial-style furnishings with laid-back, island-inspired touches. This Old House reader Liz Shivel of Huntington Beach, California, fell in love with an outdoor dining room that paired a caned daybed—scored for just $20 at a flea market—with rattan lamps and flowing curtains. Designer Trip Hoffman, who created the space, chose “elements that feel inviting but also a bit exotic,” striking the perfect balance between casual and sophisticated.

Choosing the right outdoor furniture can create a comfortable and inviting area for guests. Consider a mix of seating options to accommodate different group sizes and activities. Here are a few popular tiki bar furniture options: Adirondack chairs Bar stools Daybed Dining table and matching chairs Hammocks Hanging chairs Sectional sofa Side tables Storage ottomans Sun loungers

When selecting furniture, opt for materials that can withstand the elements, such as teak, rattan, or weather-resistant wicker. Add colorful cushions and throw pillows in tropical prints to match the tiki theme and provide extra comfort.

Pro Tip: This Old House master carpenter Norm Abram, who has built plenty of outdoor furniture over the years, is fond of teak for its durability—even though its preternatural hardness “completely knocks the edge off my planer knives.” Redwood, cedar, and cypress are also on his short list because they resist rot and insects. Include Bug Repellents at Your Tiki Bar

Keeping insects at bay is crucial for enjoying your outdoor tiki bar in the summer. Add citronella torches and these bug-repelling options to your tiki bar environment for a bug-free zone:

Bug zappers: Place these away from the main entertaining area to attract insects away from your guests.

Essential oil diffusers: Consider setting up designated bug-repellent areas with essential oil diffusers that use eucalyptus or tea tree oil. These can naturally deter pests while adding a pleasant aroma to the space.

Mosquito netting: Drape the netting around seating areas or the bar to keep mosquitoes away from food and drinks.

Mosquito-repelling plants: Incorporate lemongrass, marigolds, or lavender into your landscaping as a natural deterrent to mosquitoes.

Oscillating fans: These can keep flying insects away from seating areas.
