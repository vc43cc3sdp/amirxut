What to Consider When Choosing LED Shop Lights

Photo: Glenda Taylor for BobVila.com Photo: Glenda Taylor for BobVila.com Photo: Glenda Taylor for BobVila.com Photo: Glenda Taylor for BobVila.com

Sometimes jargon can be confusing. It can be difficult to understand what impact lumens, kelvins, volts, and watts have on specific lighting choices. In the following section, we explain each of these specifications in detail so you’ll have the information you need to make an informed decision. We also cover ease of installation and additional features that may be of interest. Brightness

Not so long ago, bulb brightness, particularly for incandescent lights, was given in watts. However, watts actually indicate power consumption. It’s a useful metric because a higher wattage means more power consumption and therefore higher bills, but it isn’t about brightness at all.

A more important metric for determining brightness is lumens (not to be confused with the popular online lighting store of the same name). It’s sometimes written as LM. Higher numbers are brighter. The number of required lumens will depend on the work involved and is usually calculated as lumens per square foot. For general purpose tasks like auto repair, a relatively low figure of around 70 lumens per square foot should be sufficient. For highly detailed tasks like working with electronic circuitry or engraving, 300 lumens per square foot or more would be appropriate. Color Temperature

Color temperature is measured in kelvins, usually just shown as K. 3,000K is at the lower end of the spectrum. It provides a white light usually associated with halogen bulbs and is often found in offices. However, for an LED work light, it can be a little harsh.

5,000K is the most common color temperature for an LED workshop light. The slight blue color is kinder on the eyes, particularly during long days at the workshop. The most powerful lighting for large spaces can measure all the way up to 6500K. The blue tone creates an atmosphere that is close to natural daylight. Installation

Most LED shop lights are relatively easy to install, though it’s important to consider positioning so that the light shines in the right places. Bear in mind that cheap task lighting can be added if you require more illumination in a specific spot, such as a workbench. Some single LED shop lights can be screwed into existing fittings. Others can be mounted directly to the ceiling, or they can be hung from chains. The latter is useful in steel-framed buildings.

Some shop lights can be plugged into a standard outlet, though hardwiring may also be an option. Those without the required installation knowledge should hire an electrician. In general, safety is an important consideration. While installation may not be complex, fitting LED lights in spaces with high ceilings presents its own dangers, so it may be worth contacting a professional. Additional Features

Some shop lights, like LED strip lights, can be linked together so they can be activated using a single switch. Pull chains are another popular way to turn them on and off.

Motion activation can be convenient, especially if you have your hands full. In some cases, sensors can be placed outside of the main shop area so that the lights automatically turn on as you arrive.

Multileaved (multipaneled) lights are practical in smaller shops and garages since they can be adjusted to spotlight a particular area or provide overall illumination. It might also be worth considering a hard hat light.

Some LED shop lights come in waterproof housings (look for the internationally-recognized IP rating). This can be valuable in areas with high humidity for where there’s a risk of water being splashed on the lights. FAQs

The article above contains detailed information to help buyers assess the best LED shop light for their particular workspace. If you’re still looking for workshop or garage lighting ideas, check out some of the common questions we are often asked.

Q.What type of light is the safest option for use in a shop?

LED shop light fixtures are generally considered the safest option for shop use for a couple of reasons. Fluorescent lights contain mercury, which is poisonous and can be released when they break. LED lights are usually less liable to break and run much cooler, so there’s little risk of burns or fires. Q.What color LED is best for working?

The best LED color for working depends to some extent on ambient light. In basement rooms where no external lighting is available, the color rating of 4000K provides cool white light suitable for concentration. That rating also works well for cabinet lighting. In more open areas such as garages and warehouses, 5000K is a popular choice, and 6500K is comparable to natural daylight.

Q. How many lumens are good for a shop light?

The right number of lumens for shop lighting will depend on the type of work taking place. Expert advice varies from around 70 lumens per square foot for typical garage lighting up to 300 lumens per square foot for task lighting or home office use. The more detailed the work, the brighter the lighting should be. Q. How long do LED shop lights last?

If you’ve wondered how long LED lights last, you can expect to get between 25,000 to 50,000 hours of illumination, depending on the quality of the lights. Q. Are LED shop lights easy to install?

Most are very easy to install, requiring only basic tools.

Q. How can you tell if a LED shop light is good quality?

When considering an LED light for shop spaces, your best bet is to look for high lumens (over 4000) and good customer reviews. You may also want to look for a weather-resistant rating if you plan to use them outdoors or in a humid garage.

Q. How many LED lights do I need for my shop?

A good estimate is to aim for 80-100 lumens per square foot. Q. Are LED shop lights worth it?

That ultimately depends on what you’re looking for and what you’re willing to pay, but in most cases, LED shop lights are energy-efficient and cost-effective. Meet the Testers
