Pro Tip: David Raymond, a designer and builder in Connecticut, points out that the kitchen office concept is a natural fit: “Every family needs a command center to deal with scheduling the day and running the house.” For households where a full home office isn’t feasible, even a stand-up message center near the kitchen can handle quick tasks like scribbling calendar commitments or setting up a lunch date during the minutes before heading off to work.

Pro Tip: Connie Edwards, design director for Shenandoah Cabinetry in Winchester, Virginia, suggests keeping a kitchen desk at counter height for maximum flexibility: “You can do homework or pay bills here, but you can also use it as a staging area for a buffet or to roll out a pie crust.” When cooking threatens to spill over into your workspace, office supplies can be quickly stowed in a drawer.

A superior setup for a living room-office combo is to arrange seating, such as a sofa and two chairs, around a coffee table, as is standard, and then situate the desk behind the sofa. An attractive desk chair on casters can roll easily into the “living” part of the space as desired. The grouping around the coffee table may need to be a bit closer than pre-WFH, but you’ll likely grow accustomed to that intimacy.

Pro Tip: Architect Duo Dickinson warns against underestimating how much room you’ll need: “Everybody makes the assumption that all they need is a little corner of a room—but usually they end up spread all over the house.” He recommends that a work space measure at least 10-by-10-feet, even when it shares a room with living areas. Dinner and a desk

The dining room can serve as a workspace—but who wants to move a computer, printer, camera, etc. off the table every evening for dinner? Set up a small desk in its own spot rather than using the table. Stow work gear in an existing hutch, sideboard, or china cabinet, or build in a banquette with under-seat storage for office supplies.

A rolling file cabinet or utility cart can be pushed into a corner or closet when it’s time to dine. If you’ve got no choice but to work at the dining table, treat yourself to a chair comfy enough to let you pull long hours there. Divide and conquer

Despite your best attempts toward an office setup that works with your home décor, mixing business with pleasure can be visually jarring. That’s where room dividers come in. Folding screens, curtains, bookcases, or simply a sheet of attractive fabric can separate the laidback aspects of a room (the bed or sofa, say) from the no-nonsense stuff (desk, office equipment).

Pro Tip: Suzanne Felber, an interior designer based in Dallas, Texas, advises: “Think of screens as temporary walls.
