If you’re debating painting your home’s exterior yourself or hiring a professional, there are a number of things to consider before you start sampling color swatches. First, it’s worth examining the scope of the project and evaluating the time and effort it will take to complete. Professional painters usually have top-notch equipment and years of experience to get exterior house painting done fast and done right, which may not be the case for a beginning or intermediate DIYer. Second, consider the budget for this project and what new materials may cost. Ladders, brushes, sprayers, scaffolding, and drop cloths can add up quickly. A third consideration to keep in mind is the potential hazards or roadblocks that may keep you from taking on the job yourself. Water damage, tricky landscaping, and dangerous angles are important elements to keep in mind when deciding whether to hire a professional.

There’s a time and a place for DIY projects, and if you’ve painted the exterior of a home before, you may want to tackle this task on your own. If your exterior is in mint condition, you’ve set aside the time it takes to paint, and you already own the necessary materials, the job may as well be yours. Taking on exterior home painting yourself will likely save money in the long run and add to your DIY resume. Plus, you won’t have to wait for a professional bid or schedule the job on someone else’s timeline.

You have experience painting a home’s exterior.

Experience is all but everything, especially in the world of home improvement. If you’ve painted the exterior of a home before, chances are you know what to expert in terms of time commitment, prep work, and end results. Prior painting experience is key to producing a job that looks professional without the high costs of labor that are often associated with hiring a pro. If you’ve learned the tricks of the trade and are willing to take on the task, your work will likely pay off in the long run and build your confidence as a seasoned DIYer.

You have enough time to allot to the painting project.

Exterior house painting can be time consuming, especially when you factor in prep work, which can account for 30 to 50 percent of the allotted time. Depending on the size of the exterior, painting may take several days and several coats of paint to complete.

Tack on the extra 24 hours or more it could take for the paint to dry, and it could take an entire weekend to complete this project. If you’ve blocked out the time it will take to prepare and paint the exterior yourself (or maybe you’ve enlisted your own crew for help), it may be time to start sampling those swatches.

You are on a tight budget and already have the necessary tools.

If you already own ladders, scaffolding, a paint sprayer, and other tools, painting the exterior of your home will likely be considerably less expensive than hiring a professional. Assuming the tools are in working condition and are suitable for the size of the paint job, fewer purchases mean you’ll be more likely to adhere to or even stay under your budget.

In this instance, the cost of paint will likely be where the majority of your money is spent. If you’re confident in your DIY skills and have the spare time to dedicate to this project, painting the exterior yourself may make the most sense.

There is no damage to the exterior of your home.

Before committing to such an extensive project, a thorough inspection of the home’s exterior may be necessary to determine whether you should hire a professional. If any signs of water damage are present, those repairs will need to be made before painting begins to ensure the paint holds up over time.

Investing in the material and gear needed will exceed your budget.

Not only will a professional already own the necessary equipment to complete the job, but they’ll likely have top-of-the-line materials and gear that a beginner might not be able to splurge on. Plus, if you only need to paint your exterior once, you may not have a need for the equipment afterward.
